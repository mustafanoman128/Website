I help businesses turn operational data into the dashboards, pipelines, and models their teams actually use. My clients come in with data that exists but isn't working. Reports that take too long to build. Pipelines that need constant manual fixes. Decisions still made on gut feel.

My training started in economics — understanding how information, incentives, and decisions shape outcomes. A Master's in Business Analytics at UIC introduced me to machine learning, and that was the pull. Feed data into a model well enough, and it tells you something the data alone couldn't. I've been building on that idea ever since.

One early project was a house price predictor for Bangalore. It worked — so I asked the obvious next question: why hasn't anyone done this for Pakistan? No platform gave buyers a data-backed estimate. The market runs on gut feel and informal networks. So I trained models on 120,000 listings and shipped it. That gap-filling instinct is how I approach client work too.

I work independently because accountability gets lost in layers. When you hire me, I'm the analyst, the engineer, and the person answering your messages. No project managers relaying requirements. No handoffs. You get the person doing the work, and when it ships, you own everything.

Three years inside US enterprise data teams — real budgets, production systems, C-suite reporting — means I know what's at stake when the numbers are wrong. When you bring me into your data environment, I treat it accordingly: fixed scope before work starts, full ownership on delivery, and results that hold up when someone important is watching.
